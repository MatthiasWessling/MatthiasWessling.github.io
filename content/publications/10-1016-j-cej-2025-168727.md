## Abstract

Electrification of temperature swing adsorption presents a sustainable approach for industrial carbon dioxide (CO ₂ ) capture, overcoming the limitations of steam-based heating and facilitating decarbonization. Current designs for electrically heated adsorbents fall short in terms of scalability, energy efficiency, and integration into industrial workflows. To address these challenges, we present a modular, spiral wound adsorption module based on a polyethyleneimine (PEI)-impregnated non-woven carbon fabric, specifically engineered for direct, uniform resistive heating. This system allows to study external heat demands and the resulting dynamical response, in particular during desorption. The carbon fabric exhibited nearly constant electrical resistivity from ambient temperature to 120 °C, facilitating precise and uniform resistive heating. In dynamic heating tests, the adsorbent in the module reached 120 °C in approximately 15 s at a specific power of 0.38 W cm ⁻². For breakthrough experiments, 15 vol.% CO ₂ at an elevated adsorption temperature of 70 °C was used to mimic flue gas conditions. The PEI-impregnated carbon fabric yielded a loading of 0.46 ± 0.03 mmol g ⁻¹. Subsequent regeneration at 120 °C released 73.5 ± 8.2 % of the captured CO ₂ in 45 min. The CO ₂ working capacity achieved in the spiral wound module under dynamic flow conditions was lower than the capacities determined by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), where maximum loadings of up to 1.09 mmol g ⁻¹ were observed within 1 h of adsorption. This discrepancy highlights the impact of adsorption and desorption kinetics, as well as diffusion limitations in the PEI layer, when transitioning from idealized TGA conditions to practical, high-throughput module operation.
