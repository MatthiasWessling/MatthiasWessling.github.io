## Abstract

Sterile filtration of lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) operates at membrane pore sizes comparable to particle dimensions, resulting in non-classical fouling behavior. However, existing sterile filtration studies rely primarily on bulk characterization methods such as dynamic light scattering (DLS), which cannot resolve transmission of individual LNP subpopulations. Here, we combine controlled constant-flux sterile filtration with asymmetric flow field-flow fractionation coupled to diode-array detection and multi-angle light scattering (AF4-DAD-MALS) to quantify, to our knowledge for the first time, population-specific transmission of LNPs during 0.1 µm pore-sized sterile filtration while simultaneously monitoring TMP evolution. Segmented Hermia analysis indicated an initial intermediate blocking regime followed by cake-filtration-like behavior. While DLS indicated preferential depletion of larger LNPs in the permeate, AF4 resolved three LNP populations in the feed and quantified their population-specific transmission, with the largest population showing lower transmission than the small and medium populations. The presented workflow facilitates population-resolved quantification of LNP transmission during sterile filtration and provides a framework to relate particle heterogeneity to hydraulic filtration behavior. This enables future mechanistic investigations of particle transport across membranes and filtration processes beyond conventional bulk characterization.
