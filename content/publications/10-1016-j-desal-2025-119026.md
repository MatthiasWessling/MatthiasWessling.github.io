## Abstract

The increasing demand for lithium, coupled with its diminishing availability, underscores the critical need for efficient lithium recovery from diverse ionic mixtures. This investigation focuses on the separation of lithium-containing multi-salt feeds combined with organic matter using polyelectrolyte multilayer (PEM) hollow fiber nanofiltration membranes fabricated via in-module layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition, which allows for easily tailored separation characteristics. Still lacking studies on multi-ionic feeds, this work elucidates the potential of hollow fiber membranes coated with poly(diallyldimethylammonium chloride) (PDADMAC) and poly(sodium 4-styrenesulfonate) (PSS) for lithium recovery from aqueous ionic mixtures of various origins, holistically characterizing their separation of monovalent and multivalent ions. An emerging lithium resource are lithium-ion batteries (LIBs). In a new recycling approach, wet shredding of spent LIBs produces an aqueous phase at pH 8, which is analyzed for lithium, sodium, fluoride, sulfate, and other ions, as well as dissolved organics. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to analyze the evolving solution and explore the application of membranes in the wet shredding recycling process of LIBs. Addressing anion separation challenges like in LIB recycling processes, the (PDADMAC/PSS) 8 membrane separates a two-salt mixture with 5% fluoride and 97% sulfate rejection. This separation trend is also contained when testing an actual wet shredding solution despite the presence of dissolved organic matter. Examining the cation separation in lithium mining from salt lake brines, the membrane showcases notable negative lithium rejections up to -56%. The addition of multivalent cations enhances the negative rejection of lithium, emphasizing the membrane’s potential for both ion separation and simultaneous lithium concentration. The study highlights the membrane’s versatility for both anion and cation separation, offering a strong foundation for further tuning PDADMAC/PSS-based hollow fiber NF membranes for targeted lithium recovery applications.
