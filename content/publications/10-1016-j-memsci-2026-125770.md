## Abstract

Ion-transport barriers in nanofiltration membranes are often rationalized in terms of steric exclusion, Donnan exclusion and dehydration, yet their molecular origins remain difficult to resolve in heterogeneous charged polymer networks. Here, we combine molecular dynamics (MD) free-energy calculations with machine learning (ML) to identify the local ion-environment descriptors governing transport barriers in a poly(styrenesulfonate)/ poly(diallyldimethylammonium) polyelectrolyte membrane. Umbrella-sampling simulations were used to reconstruct free-energy profiles for sodium and chloride transport, while the local ion environment was characterized by first- and second-shell coordination and hydration-shell geometry. Both ions exhibit significant barriers within the membrane, accompanied by partial dehydration, reduced hydration-shell size, and increased shell distortion. However, these hydration-based descriptors alone do not uniquely determine the local free energy. Notably, some weakly hydrated configurations exhibit comparatively favorable free energies when water loss is compensated by coordination with membrane functional groups and nearby ions. XGBoost models combined with SHAP analysis show that, within the sampled permeation pathways, the local free-energy penalty is shaped by the coupled interplay of hydration, steric confinement, ion pairing, and polymer coordination. Nearby counterions and oppositely charged membrane groups appear especially important in reducing dehydration-associated free-energy costs. These findings supply a pathway-resolved molecular interpretation of ion-transport barriers in polyelectrolyte membranes. They further demonstrate that combined MD–ML analysis can reveal the main descriptors governing free-energy variations in heterogeneous charged membrane environments.
