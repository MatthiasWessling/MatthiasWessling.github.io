## Abstract

Understanding flow distribution in flat-sheet membrane modules is essential for ensuring uniform transmembrane flux, predictable mass transfer, and reliable device performance. Often, the opaque housing and complex permeate structures prevent direct observation of internal hydrodynamics. Here, we used low-field magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), tracer-based residence time analysis, and validated computational fluid dynamics (CFD) to resolve flow and pressure fields inside two commercial modules that differ solely in their permeate channel topology: one with disconnected channels and one with fully connected channels. The disconnected topology yielded a highly uniform, unidirectional permeate flow and superior self-deaeration, resulting in stable and homogeneous transmembrane flux. In contrast, the connected topology produced flow reversal, stagnation zones, and pressure peaks in the outer channels which intensified at higher flow rates due to mixing-induced pressure losses. However, its effective membrane area is more resilient to entrapped air compared to the disconnected channel design. Tracer experiments and simulations further revealed pronounced inlet-dominated membrane usage in both designs and a broadened residence time distribution in the connected-channel module. These results demonstrate that permeate-side architecture is a determinant of flow homogeneity, air management, and effective membrane utilization. The combined MRI–CFD approach provides a generalizable framework for diagnosing hidden mal-distribution in opaque membrane devices and establishes design criteria for improving future sterile filtration modules.
