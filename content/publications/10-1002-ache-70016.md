## Abstract

ABSTRACT Electrochemical decarboxylation converts carboxylic acids into hydrocarbons without oxidants or high temperatures. Although reaction mechanisms are increasingly understood, studies in scalable reactor architectures under industrially relevant conditions remain limited. Here, propionic acid serves as a model substrate to investigate electrochemical decarboxylation in a 25 zero‐gap flow cell, targeting the non‐Kolbe product ethylene. The interplay of anode material, electrolyte composition, membrane, and flow conditions is systematically evaluated at 300 mA cm −2. Notably, optimal pH differs for platinum and boron‐doped diamond catalysts. Commercial bipolar membranes degrade during high‐rate operation, leading to progressively increasing cell potentials reaching 15 V, with an average ethylene selectivity of 53%. A shared‐electrolyte configuration enables the use of cation exchange membranes, resulting in stable cell potentials of 5.6 V at comparable ethylene selectivity (46%). Using anion exchange membranes, we reveal that selectivity trends for platinum catalysts in conventional gap cells are not directly transferable to zero‐gap architectures. This is attributed to the altered local reaction environment caused by accumulation of and (bi)carbonate species at the anolyte–membrane interface, where the anode is located in zero‐gap systems. Our findings underscore the importance of scalable flow cell studies and guide rational process design.
